## Pre-registration (committed before any code of this task was written and before any run)

No exploratory run was made. The numbers below are arithmetic from the formulas. Every case runs under the reference law **L0**, so every statement will be **mathematics**: a check of a numerical method, not a finding about nature.

**The three candidates of question 12**
- **A, nearest image (D36, to be built in the engine):** each pair of bodies interacts once, through the closest of its periodic copies; positions are wrapped back into the box.
- **B, open-space forces:** positions wrap, forces ignore the copies. Written in the scenario script only.
- **C, the full periodic sum:** every copy acts. Written in the scenario script only, as a plain sum over the copies in the square shells `|i|, |j| <= n`, for a pair with no net charge (for which the sum converges), at `n` = 50, 100, 200.

**Settings common to all:** scaled units (`G_N = K = 1`, `s = 1`), 2D (motion in a plane, `1/r^2` field: D21), `phi_bar` = 90 degrees, a square box of side `L = 1` with corners (0, 0) and (1, 1).

### D36.1: the force on one body of a pair (forces evaluated once)
- **Setup:** two bodies with charges +1 and -1 and masses 1e-12 (so that Newton's part of the force is 1e-24 of Coulomb's), on the line y = 0.5.
  - (a) straddling the edge: at x = 0.02 and x = 0.98. Their true separation through the edge is 0.04.
  - (b) inside the box, centred, at separations `d` = 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4.
- **Predictions:**
  - (a) A gives the attraction `1 / 0.04^2 = 625` to 1e-12. B sees the separation 0.96 and gives `1 / 0.96^2 = 1.085`, 1.7e-3 of the right force.
  - (b) A and B are identical (the pair does not straddle the edge). Against C: the copies of the pair are a square lattice of dipoles `p = d`. To leading order they make a uniform field `p S / (2 L^3)` along the dipole at the pair, with `S = sum 1/R^3` over the square lattice `= 4 zeta(3/2) beta(3/2) = 9.0336`. It pushes the positive charge away from the negative one, so **A overestimates the attraction by the fraction `4.517 (d/L)^3`**: 5.6e-4, 4.5e-3, 3.6e-2 and 0.29 at the four separations. The next term is of relative order `(d/L)^2` of this one.
  - C has converged: the value at `n` = 200 differs from `n` = 100 by less than 1e-3 of the correction itself.
- **Pass:** (a) as stated. (b) `(F_A - F_C) / F_A` is positive and within 20% of `4.517 (d/L)^3` at `d` = 0.05 and 0.1, and within a factor 2 at 0.2. At `d` = 0.4 it is reported, not judged (the leading term is not enough there).

### D36.2: Newton's third law
- **Setup:** the gas of D36.4 at its initial state.
- **Prediction:** A and B are both pairwise, so under both the sum of all forces is zero to rounding: below 1e-12 of the sum of the sizes of the forces. The third law does **not** tell A from B. (C would pass too; it is not computed for 40 bodies.)
- **Pass:** both residuals below 1e-12.

### D36.3: a bound pair carried across the edge
- **Setup:** two neutral bodies of mass 1 on a circular orbit of separation `a = 0.02` (relative speed `sqrt(2 / a) = 10`, period `2 pi a / 10 = 0.012566`), centred at (0.5, 0.5), with a common velocity (5, 0) added. Softening 0, the adaptive step of D29 with `eta = 1e-3`, `t_end = 0.35`. The centre of mass crosses the edge at t = 0.1 and t = 0.3; each time the pair straddles the edge for a third of a period. Reference: the same pair in open space (no box), same common velocity.
- **Predictions:**
  - A: the pair does not notice the edge. The separation stays `a` within 1e-5 throughout; the energy (computed with the nearest image) has end drift and largest excursion below 1e-6; the final relative position agrees with the open-space run to 1e-6 of `a`; the step count is within 1% of the open-space run.
  - B: at the first crossing the two bodies see each other at a distance of about `L` and fly apart: the separation at the end, measured through the nearest image, exceeds `10 a`.
- **Pass:** all of the above.

### D36.4: a gas in a periodic box
- **Setup:** 40 bodies of mass 1, 20 with charge +0.05 and 20 with -0.05, places uniform in the box and velocities normal with spread 0.3 on each axis, drawn with seed 11; softening 0.02; adaptive step, `eta = 1e-3`; 4000 steps. Run under A by the engine's runner (the stamped run), and under A and B by the script's own loop (to count the crossings and to give B the same treatment).
- **Predictions:**
  - A: mass and charge exactly constant; every body inside the box at the end; momentum drift below 1e-12 (the forces are pairwise); at least 20 edge crossings in the run (speed 0.3 times the elapsed time, of order 0.5, in a box of side 1). Energy with the nearest image: end drift below 1e-3. This number is the least certain: the nearest-image force of an unscreened `1/r^2` field jumps when a pair's closest copy changes (at a separation of half the box along an axis), and velocity Verlet does not like jumps. This is the "stated limit" of D36, and the run measures its size.
  - B: the energy (open-space formula) jumps at each crossing; its end drift is at least 10 times that of A.
  - The loop of the script and the engine's runner agree for A: same final positions to 1e-9.
- **Pass:** all of the above. A failed prediction is reported as such.

**Analysis plan:** the quantities above, each with its flag, in one `summary.json` per test (`results/D36.1` to `results/D36.4`), written by `scenarios/D36/run.py`. End drift and largest excursion are both reported (D21.3).

**What a pass would and would not mean:** it would show that the engine's nearest image does what D36 says and that B is not a usable alternative, and it would put a number on A's error against the full sum for one simple case. It would not show that the nearest image is adequate for a given physical study: that depends on the box being much larger than the structures studied, which each scenario must argue for itself.

- **Changes to the plan (with date and reason):**
  - 2026-11-14, **before any run of the scenario**, D36.3: the energy drift cannot be taken relative to the total energy. With the common velocity (5, 0) I chose, each body has the speed `sqrt(50)`, the kinetic energy is 50 and the binding energy is `-1 / 0.02 = -50`: the total is exactly zero. I saw it in a unit test of the engine part (a pair with the same numbers gave a "relative drift" of 1e6), not in a scenario run. The setup stays as pre-registered; the **scale** of every energy drift in the script becomes the interaction energy at the start (`|U_0|`): drift = `|E - E_0| / |U_0|`. The thresholds (1e-6 in D36.3, 1e-3 and the factor 10 in D36.4) are unchanged and now refer to that scale. For D36.4, where the total energy is not near zero, this changes the number by a factor of order one. The runner's own `conservation.energy_re` (relative to the total energy) is still reported for the gas.

### D36.5: a gas whose bodies do cross the edge (pre-registered on 2026-11-14, after the results of D36.1 to D36.4, before any run of it)

**Why a fifth test.** D36.4 ran as pre-registered and its gas made **no** edge crossing in 4000 steps: 40 bodies of mass 1 in a box of side 1 attract each other so strongly (interaction energy 2.6e3 against a kinetic energy of 3.6) that the adaptive step is tiny and the run covers almost no time. My estimate "an elapsed time of order 0.5" ignored that. So D36.4 says nothing about A against B in a gas, which issue #58 asked for. D36.5 is that test with a gas dilute enough to cross. I have seen the results of D36.1 to D36.4 and nothing else: no run of D36.5 or of anything like it was made.

- **Setup:** as D36.4 (40 bodies, places and velocities drawn with seed 11, spread 0.3, softening 0.02, `eta = 1e-3`, box of side 1), but masses 1e-3, charges +2e-3 (20 bodies) and -2e-3 (20 bodies), and 8000 steps.
- **Estimates (arithmetic only):** the mean spacing is `1 / sqrt(40) = 0.16` and a typical nearest-neighbour distance about 0.08. The accelerations are of order `m / r^2 = 0.16`, so the step is set by the geometric conditions of the rule (`eta r / |v|`, about 2e-4), not by the forces: 8000 steps cover a time of order 1, in which a body at speed 0.4 crosses the box about 0.4 times per axis. Kinetic energy `40 x 0.5 x 1e-3 x 0.18 = 3.6e-3`; interaction energy of order 1e-3 (gravity of 780 pairs at about 0.5; the charges add terms of both signs).
- **Predictions:**
  - A (the engine's runner and the script's loop): mass and charge exactly constant; every body inside the box at the end; momentum drift below 1e-12; **at least 20 edge crossings**; energy end drift and largest excursion, on the scale of the interaction energy at the start, below 1e-3.
  - B (the script's loop): the energy (open-space formula) jumps at crossings; its largest excursion is at least 10 times that of A, and above 1e-2.
  - The loop and the runner agree for A: same final positions to 1e-9.
- **Pass:** all of the above. As before, a failed prediction is reported as such.

## What was built (engine parts)

- `environment.py`: the boundary `"wrap"`. A body that leaves through one face comes back through the opposite one; fixed and absorbed bodies are not moved; no wall is hit, so nothing is given to a wall. `Environment.period` gives the sides of the box.
- `fields/direct.py`: `nearest_image(r, period)` and `DirectSum(period=...)`: every displacement between bodies goes through the closest periodic copy. The same optional `period` in `smallest_separation`, in the energies (`energy.py`, `diagnostics.py`) and in the distances of the step rule (`integrators.py`), so that force, energy and step agree. With no period nothing changes: every earlier test passes unchanged.
- `universe.py`: `Universe.period`; an inflow is refused in a wrap-around box (it enters through a wall, and there is none).
- `runner.py`: passes the period; **`summary["wrap_around"]` carries the stated limit of D36 in every result of a wrap-around box**; no wall pressure there.
- Choices, labelled: the Composer position `xc` of D37 is not wrapped (it is not a place in the box); a uniform background field is allowed in a wrap-around box, but its energy term is not periodic, so the energy then jumps at each crossing (a driven system); the screening length of L8 is still taken from the extent of the bodies, not from the box.
- 8 unit tests (`engine/tests/test_wrap_around.py`).

Statements, labelled:

- **Mathematics (the engine does what D36 says).** Through the edge a pair feels its true separation to rounding; a bound pair carried across the edge twice cannot be told from the same pair in open space (2.8e-10 of its size, same step count); forces are pairwise; mass, charge and momentum are conserved to rounding in a gas; the energy is conserved across crossings to 2.7e-7 of the interaction energy.
- **Mathematics (B is not usable).** With open-space forces a bound pair is destroyed at its first crossing, and a gas loses 10% of its interaction energy in 8 crossings.
- **Mathematics (the size of the stated limit, for one case).** For a pair with no net charge of size `d` in a box of side `L`, the nearest image overestimates the attraction by `4.517 (d/L)^3` to leading order: 0.06% at `d/L` = 0.05, 0.5% at 0.1, 4% at 0.2, 41% at 0.4. This is the price of cutting the `1/r^2` sum at half the box, and it is what "boxes much larger than the structures studied" means in numbers.

**After the run (not part of any verdict).** The three stored sums of D36.1 differ in the ratio 1.985, as an error proportional to `1/n` would (2). Extrapolating them to infinitely many shells (`2 S_200 - S_100`) gives an excess of 1.006, 1.023, 1.094 and 1.430 times the leading term at the four separations, stable to 5e-5 against the extrapolation from 50 and 100. The deviations from 1 grow as about `2.3 (d/L)^2`, the order the pre-registration gave for the next term. This is arithmetic on the stored numbers, made after seeing them.

**What I got wrong, plainly.** Three estimates in my pre-registrations: how fast a square-shell sum converges, and twice how much time a gas run would cover. None concerns the method under test, but they are the reason the verdicts read "fail", and I have not adjusted a criterion or re-run a test to make them read otherwise. D36.5 exists because D36.4 turned out not to test what it was for; it was pre-registered again before it ran.
