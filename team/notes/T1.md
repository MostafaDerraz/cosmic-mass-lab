## Pre-registration (committed before any code of T1 is written, and before any run)

Written on 2026-11-14. The numbers in "Predictions" are plain arithmetic of the two-body problem (a scratch script; no engine code was run on any T1 case for this pre-registration).

### The cases

SI units, 2D (planar motion, D21), frame angle 79.86 degrees, open space, no background, **softening 0** (D21: two-body cases). The two bodies start on the x axis in their centre-of-mass frame, the first at negative x; the relative velocity is along +y.

| T1.2a | Earth, proton | 7e6 m | 0 | `s q g`: pull, push or swirl? |

| T1.3b | proton, electron | 1e-10 m | 0 | the same, for opposite charges |
| T1.4 | proton, electron | 5.29177e-11 m (Bohr radius) | `v_ref` | Bound? Precession? Spiral? Binding energy? |
| T1.5 | two Stoney bodies `m_S + e G`, `m_S = e s` | 1 m | 0 | No interaction under L0. What under the other laws? |
| T1.6 | proton, electron | 1e-10 m | 0.7 `v_ref` | Newton's third law: force on 1 = -(force on 2)? |

- **Matter:** the presets of the test plan. The Stoney mass is `e s` computed from the engine's constants, so that `Re(mu1 mu2) = m_S^2 - s^2 e^2` is zero to rounding (the table's rounded 1.85921e-9 kg would leave 1e-6 of the force).
- **Radii (D46):** Earth 6.371e6 m, moon 1.7374e6 m, proton 0.84e-15 m; the electron is a point. The Stoney body is a point (labelled choice: D46 names no radius for it).
- **Reference scales of a case, the same for every law** (so that the protocol does not depend on the law under test): `k_ref = G_N m_a m_b + K |q_a q_b|`, the reduced mass `m_red`, `a_ref = k_ref / (m_red r0^2)`, `v_ref = sqrt(k_ref / (m_red r0))`, `T_ref = 2 pi sqrt(m_red r0^3 / k_ref)`. Where the pair attracts under L0 (every case with motion), `v_ref` is L0's circular speed and `T_ref` its period.
- **T1.6** (labelled choice): the third law is measured on **every case above** (the force on each body from the other alone, `laws.third_law_residual`, at the first and the last state of each run), and T1.6 adds one case of its own in which both bodies move on an eccentric orbit, so that the directions that depend on the velocity (L4) act too. Its dynamical consequence, the drift of the total momentum, is reported for every run.

### The protocol (the same for every law)

1. **Numerics:** velocity Verlet, adaptive step with `eta = 1e-3` (D10, D29), `dt_max = T_ref / 500` (only so that a pair with no force at all has a step; a circular orbit under L0 steps at `T_ref / 6283`).
2. **A run ends at the first of these events**, looked for after every step, on the Real positions `x` (D49):
   - **contact:** the separation is at most `R_a + R_b` (only T1.2: 0.910143 `r0`, the proton or electron at the Earth's surface; the field of a point Earth is not followed inside the Earth);
   - **plunge:** the separation is at most `r0 / 10` (when that is larger than `R_a + R_b`);
   - **escape:** the separation is at least `10 r0`;
   - **three revolutions:** the relative position has turned by `6 pi`, in either sense;
   - **time cap:** `t = 20 T_ref` with none of the above ("no event");
   - **step cap:** 60,000 steps at `eta = 1e-3` (120,000 and 240,000 at `eta/2` and `eta/4`): "not resolved".
3. **How it is run, with the engine's runner unchanged:** a first pass (`runner.run` with every step recorded) finds the step `n` at which the event happens; the result is the plain run of the same settings with `numerics.steps = n`. So the stamp of a T1 run reproduces it with `python -m cosmicmass run` alone, and the batch runner and the classifier of T2 read an ordinary run folder. The script checks that the two passes end in the same state, bit for bit.
4. **Convergence:** every run is repeated at `eta/2` and `eta/4`; the event, its time and the separation at the end must agree (below).

### What is measured (every run)

- the **first acceleration** of the second body relative to the first, in units of `a_ref`: its radial part (negative: **pull**; positive: **push**) and its tangential part (**swirl**); "none" if both are below 1e-12;
- the **event** and its time; the smallest and largest separation; the largest speed of a body, as a fraction of `c` (the engine is not relativistic: a speed above `c` is reported as such);
- for runs that turn: the time of each revolution, and the **closure error** after each, `sqrt((r/r0 - 1)^2 + (r'/v_ref)^2)` when the relative position has turned by `2 pi k`: 0 for an orbit that closes; the apsides (extremes of the separation) and the **apsidal precession** per radial period;
- **conservation** (the runner's summary): mass, charge, momentum, the L0 energy `energy_re`, and the energy the law conserves `energy_total` where it is defined (D23); the drift of the orbital angular momentum, in units of `m_red r0 v_ref`;
- the **binding energy** at the start, `-(energy_total)` or `-(energy_re)` where no energy is defined for the law, in eV;
- the **third-law residual** `|F_ab + F_ba| / max(|F_ab|, |F_ba|)` at the first and last state;
- the **spin** of each body at the end (D46; zero unless the direction is "torque"); under L1e the Composer position `xc` and `x - s^2 (q/m) xc` of each body beside `x` (D49).

### Predictions

`cos(phi_bar) = 0.176053996`, `1 - 2 cos(phi_bar) = 0.647892008`, `s = 1.16042704e10 kg/C`, `m_S = 1.85920909e-9 kg`. Under a central force `-k r / r^3` between the two bodies: L0 has `k = G_N m_a m_b - K q_a q_b` (compendium); L1a with all terms has `k = G_N [Re + s Com](mu_a mu_b) = G_N m_a m_b - (1 - 2 cos(phi_bar)) K q_a q_b + G_N s (m_a q_b + q_a m_b)` (D23, D48). `f = k / k_ref`. From rest, a pull reaches `x r0` after `sqrt(m_red r0^3 / 2k) [sqrt(x (1 - x)) + acos(sqrt(x))]`, and a push after `sqrt(m_red r0^3 / 2|k|) [sqrt(x (x - 1)) + ln(sqrt(x) + sqrt(x - 1))]`. An orbit started at `r0` with the speed `beta v_ref` is bound if `beta^2 < 2 f`, with the other apsis at `beta^2 r0 / (2f - beta^2)` and the period `(f / (2f - beta^2))^1.5 T_ref / sqrt(f)`.

### Analysis plan

- **L0 (mathematics).** A case passes if: the event is the predicted one; its time (for orbits: the time of each revolution) is within **1e-4** of the value above; an orbit's closure error is below **1e-3** after each revolution; the momentum drift and the drift of `energy_re` are below **1e-6** (`team/RESULTS-STANDARD.md`); mass and charge are exact; the third-law residual is below 1e-12. T1.5: no event, largest change of the separation below 1e-9.
- **L1a (finding of the virtual universe, law L1a; a hypothesis, D1, and excluded at lambda = 1 by measurement, T9.1).** Each prediction above is marked "holds" or "fails" with the same tolerances, and `energy_total` (D23) takes the place of `energy_re`. A prediction that fails is a result to report, not to repair.
- **Convergence** (both laws): the event is the same at `eta`, `eta/2` and `eta/4`, and its time and the separation at the end change by less than **1%** from `eta/2` to `eta/4` (standard); the changes are reported.
- **Verdict of each test:** `finding`, with the flags above; `fail` if a case under L0 fails (then the protocol or the engine is wrong, and nothing is concluded about L1a).
- **Answers:** each of the six questions is answered yes, no, impossible or unclear for L0 and for L1a, from the measured numbers only.
- **Not in this pre-registration:** the 102 definitions (T2.2 runs them on these cases; T2's own pre-registration, `team/notes/T2.md`, judges them) and the angle sweep (T3.1).

### Changes to the plan (with date and reason)

All of 2026-11-14, written **before any production run**. Changes 1 to 3 and 6 were made while writing the code, before any run at all. Changes 4 and 5 came after the first trial runs of the script (exploratory: single runs of each case at `eta = 1e-3`, a scan of the step from 1e-2 to 2.5e-4 on T1.3a and T1.3b under L0, and one dry run of T1.3; on a tree with uncommitted files, written nowhere and never to be cited); what those runs showed is said with each change, so that the reader can judge it.

1. **The time of an event** is where the separation (or the turned angle) passes its threshold inside the last step, by linear interpolation between the two rows around it. Reason: the run itself ends on a whole step, up to 1e-3 of the time after the threshold, which is coarser than the 1e-4 the times are judged to.
2. **The first acceleration:** a part (radial, tangential) counts when it is above 1e-12 of `a_ref` and above 1e-12 of the whole acceleration. Reason: under L1a the pull on a proton is 1e18 `a_ref`; its rounding must not be read as a swirl.
3. **Tolerances not written above:** `f`, an apsis and a binding energy are judged to 1e-4, as the times are.
4. **Where the energy starts at exactly zero** (T1.5: at rest, and no interaction energy under L0) the runner reports no relative drift. There the change of the energy is reported in units of `k_ref / r0`, and judged against the same 1e-6. Seen in the trial run of T1.5 under L0 (`energy_re: null`).
5. **The energy criterion (1e-6) is judged at the finest step of the convergence series, `eta/4`,** and the drift at `eta` and `eta/2` is reported beside it, with its ratio from one step to the next. Reason: the trial runs gave an energy drift of **7.1e-6** at `eta = 1e-3` for the three runs that plunge to `r0/10` (T1.3b under L0 and L1a, T1.5 under L1a; the same number, because the three are the same problem in different units), against 5e-9 for the falls to the Earth's surface, 1.5e-7 for the escapes and below 1e-9 for the orbits. The event times of the same runs agreed with the arithmetic to 1e-8. In the scan the drift of the plunge fell with the square of the step (7.1e-6, 1.8e-6, 4.4e-7 at `eta`, `eta/2`, `eta/4`). So the adaptive step of D10 at `eta = 1e-3` does not hold the energy of a deep plunge to the standard's 1e-6, under any law, L0 included. The tolerance is **not** loosened (that would need a decision); the step is refined. If the drift at `eta/4` is still above 1e-6, or does not fall with the step, the case fails as written. The consequence for T2 (D43 asks for 1e-6 on each of T1.1-T1.6, and the batch runs at `eta = 1e-3`) is question 31 of `team/QUESTIONS.md`.
6. **The step cap** is never below the 10,001 steps that the time cap needs at `dt_max`. It changes nothing at `eta = 1e-3` or below (60,000 and more); it matters only for the coarse steps of the tests.
