## Goal
Decision D21 fixed four numerical conventions of the engine. Instead of settling them by opinion, each one is run here against its alternatives, and the numbers decide. All four are questions about the numerics of the engine under the reference law L0, so every result is **mathematics** (a check of the engine against closed forms), not a finding about nature.

| Scenario | Convention of D21 | Alternatives compared |
|---|---|---|
| D21.1 | "2D" is planar motion of point bodies of 3D space, with a `1/r^2` field | the same orbits in a full 3D run, in a tilted plane |
| D21.2 | two-body gate tests run with softening 0 | D10's softening (1e-3 of the smallest initial separation) |
| D21.3 | "energy drift" is the change at the end of the run | the largest excursion during the run; eta = 1e-3, 3e-4, 1e-4; e = 0 and 0.9 |
| D21.4 | speed floor for bodies at rest: `REST_FRACTION = 0.1` of the free-fall speed | 0 (D10 as written), 0.01, 0.1, 1.0 |

## Pre-registration (committed before any production run)

- **Law:** L0 only; SI units; static field; velocity Verlet with the adaptive step of D10.
- **D21.1 setup:** T0.1a (Earth and moon, 3.844e8 m, circular) and T0.1b (proton and electron, Bohr radius, circular), one period, eta = 1e-3, softening 0: once in 2D, once in 3D with the orbit plane tilted by rotations of 30 deg about x and 50 deg about z.
  - **Prediction:** the periods and energy drifts agree to rounding (relative difference below 1e-9), because a 1/r^2 field confined to a plane is exactly the 3D problem. **Mathematics, stated beside the runs:** a "true 2D" field falling as 1/r needs a two-dimensional constant of gravitation with other units than `G_N`; the compendium has none, so that alternative cannot be written in the language of the cosmic mass without a new derivation.
- **D21.2 setup:** T0.1a, T0.1b (one period) and T0.1c (two protons at rest, 1e-10 m, three natural times), eta = 1e-3, with softening 0 and with D10's softening.
  - **Prediction:** the Plummer softening weakens the force at the initial separation by the factor `(1 + 1e-6)^(-3/2)`, about `1 - 1.5e-6`. The orbits still pass T0.1's 0.1% criterion, but their error against the analytic period rises from about 3e-7 to about 1e-6. T0.1c (criterion 1e-6) is likely to **fail** with softening. So: softening 0 is needed for two-body gates that are compared with closed forms; D10's softening stays for many-body runs.
- **D21.3 setup:** Earth and moon, released at apocentre 3.844e8 m with eccentricity e = 0 and e = 0.9, one Kepler period, eta = 1e-3, 3e-4, 1e-4, energy recorded at every step.
  - **Prediction:** the end drift is below 1e-6 for every case. The largest excursion is below 1e-6 for e = 0, but about 4e-6 for e = 0.9 at eta = 1e-3 (MVP-6 reported 3.7e-6), and falls as eta^2 (about 3e-7 at 3e-4). So "drift at the end" and "largest excursion" lead to different verdicts at e = 0.9; both should be reported, with the criterion stated.
- **D21.4 setup:** REST_FRACTION = 0 (D10 as written), 0.01, 0.1, 1.0 on two starts from rest with closed forms: (c) two protons flying apart (T0.1c, three natural times); (f) Earth and moon falling towards each other from 3.844e8 m, to half their fall time.
  - **Prediction:** 0 gives no time step at all (the run cannot start: "impossible"). 0.01 and 0.1 give errors well below 1e-6 at a few hundred to a few thousand steps; 1.0 is coarser at the start, error perhaps 1e-6 to 1e-5. The floor only matters in the first steps, so the results above 0 differ little.
- **Analysis plan:** each scenario writes `results/D21.x/<run-id>/` (stamp, summary, time series). The summary gives each number, the prediction, and one sentence: which convention the numbers support. A prediction that fails is reported as such.
