### The rule of D43 ("conserving"), made exact

A definition is **conserving** when every one of its runs (T0.2 and T1.1-T1.6, at every frame angle of D44) meets (a), (b) and (c):

- **(a) mass and charge:** the drifts `conservation.mass` and `conservation.charge` of the run's `summary.json` are exactly 0. Absorbed bodies stay counted (E5.1), so nothing else is allowed.
- **(b) momentum:** `momentum_with_walls_drift` (the bodies' momentum plus what the walls took) is at most **1e-6**.
- **(c) energy:**
  - inside the scope of **D23** (L1a, all terms, radial, inertia `Re`, no `B_cal`): the run's drift `conservation.energy_total` (E9: Real plus Composer energy) is at most **1e-6**;
  - outside it, where no energy is defined: the **closed-loop work test** below gives a ratio of at most **1e-9**, at every one of the six angles of D44.

If a run is missing or failed, the definition is **incomplete**, not classed.

### The closed-loop work test (outside D23's scope)

A force field has an energy only if it does no work round a closed loop. The test carries a test body round two square loops in the field of two fixed sources, and integrates the work of the definition's force.

- **Units and frame:** scaled units (`G_N = K = 1`, so `s = 1`), 2D, softening 0. In scaled units the Composer terms are of the same size as the Real ones, so every term is exercised, which SI would hide behind a factor of about 1e19.
- **Sources (fixed):** `S1 = (m 1.0, q 0.3)` at (0, 0); `S2 = (m 0.5, q -0.4)` at (3, 1).
- **Test body:** `m 0.2, q 0.15`, a charge-to-mass ratio different from both sources, so that the Coupling term and the charge share are not zero.
- **Loops:** loop 1, the square with corners (1, 1.5), (2, 1.5), (2, 2.5), (1, 2.5), which encloses no source; loop 2, the square with corners (-1, -1), (1, -1), (1, 1), (-1, 1), which encloses `S1`. Both are run anticlockwise.
- **Velocity:** along the path, of unit speed, because the direction "velocity" (L4) depends on it. Everything else is static: no second field.
- **Integration:** each side by 64-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature of `F · t` (the force on the test body, from the law's `forces(...).total()`, dotted with the unit tangent). `W_loop` is the sum of the four sides.
- **Ratio:** `|W_loop| / max(|W_side|)`, the work round the loop against the largest work along one side. **0** if the force is zero everywhere on the loop.
- **Pass:** the larger of the two loops' ratios is at most **1e-9**, at each of the six angles.

The definition's strength is λ = 1 (D47). The inertia option does not change the force, except under L1e, where it scales it by a constant.

### The rule of D44 ("angle-free"), made exact

A conserving definition is in **class A** if every run's Real-coordinate results are the same at the six angles of T0.3 (10, 45, 79.86, 90, 135, 170 degrees). Otherwise it is in **class B**. "The same" means a relative difference of at most **1e-9** between the largest and the smallest value at the six angles, for:
- **T1.1-T1.6:** the final positions and velocities of every body (the last row of `timeseries.csv`);
- **T0.2:** the final `energy_re`, `kinetic` and the components of the momentum in `summary.json`, because the time series of a run of 200 bodies holds no positions.

Each relative difference is taken against the largest absolute value of that quantity over the six angles. A quantity that is 0 at every angle counts as the same.

A definition that does not conserve is in **class C**, and the table reports which of (a), (b), (c) failed, and where.

### What the classifier reads (the interface T2.2 writes to)

One folder per run, written by the runner as for any run (`stamp.json`, `summary.json`, `timeseries.csv`):

    results/T2/<definition-id>/<setup>/phi<angle>/<run-id>/

with `<setup>` one of `T0.2`, `T1.1` to `T1.6`, and `<angle>` one of `10`, `45`, `79.86`, `90`, `135`, `170`. The stamp's `law` block must equal the definition's block in `scenarios/T2/definitions.json`, with the angle taken from the stamp's `universe.phi_bar_deg`. The classifier checks both and refuses a run that does not match. When a setup has more than one run of a definition and angle, the newest counts.

## T2.3: results of the closed-loop work test

Run `results/T2.3/20261114-221506-36308dd` (engine commit `36308dd`, a clean committed tree), from `python scenarios/T2/loop_test.py`, after the pre-registration (`85aabca`), the classifier (`ff4d69b`) and the script were committed. **Mathematics** (a property of each definition as a numerical law); the readings are hypotheses (D1).

- **All 102 predictions held** (verdict `pass`): **48 definitions pass** the test at all six angles, **54 fail**, family by family exactly as predicted.
- **Passing:** the radial L1a, L1b, L1c and L1e (24) and every torque of L1a-L1d (24). The largest ratio is **1.0e-15**: rounding.
- **Failing:** every perpendicular and velocity definition (48) and the radial L1d (6). Their ratios at the angles where they fail are of order **1 to 4**: the work round the loop is as large as the work along one side.
- **The expected exception:** the 12 L1b definitions with the directions perpendicular and velocity pass at 90 degrees alone (ratios 2e-15), where `cos 90° = 0` and L1b adds nothing. They fail at the other five angles, so they fail the test.
- **Margin:** between rounding (about 1e-15) and the failures (about 1), the threshold 1e-9 of D43 cannot be crossed by rounding.

These ratios are condition (c) of D43 for the **100 definitions outside D23's scope**. The **2 inside it** (`L1a+radial.Re` and `L1a-radial.Re`) are judged by the energy of their runs; they pass the loop test too, as D23's derivation says they must. The classes A, B and C need T2.2's runs for (a), (b) and the angle rule of D44.
